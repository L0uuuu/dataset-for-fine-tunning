You are helping me generate high-quality training data to fine-tune Gemma 4 E4B

I will upload a Tunisian legal source (PDF, typically a code — e.g., Code des

YOUR TASK
For each PDF I upload, generate ONE batch of 20 training examples in valid JSON, 
randomly sampled across different articles/sections of the code for broad coverage.

BATCH COMPOSITION (20 examples total)

Language split:
- 10 examples in French
- 10 examples in Arabic (natural Modern Standard Arabic, NOT translated from French)
- Include 2 cross-lingual examples (question in one language, source article in 
  the other, answer in the question's language)

Question pattern mix (aim for roughly this distribution):
- 6 direct grounded Q&A — factual questions with specific answers from one article
- 4 multi-article synthesis — questions requiring combining 2–3 related articles
- 3 principle + exception — questions where the answer states a rule AND exceptions
- 3 refusal examples — plausible questions the code does NOT actually answer; 
  assistant should politely decline and recommend consulting a lawyer
- 2 clarification examples — ambiguous questions requiring clarification before 
  answering
- 2 procedural/lifecycle questions — questions about sequential phases or processes

Realistic user personas — questions should sound like real Tunisian users:
- Citizens (everyday concerns, often in Arabic)
- Business owners / entrepreneurs (often in French)
- HR managers, administrators (mixed)
- Students (either language, more academic framing)
DO NOT write artificial textbook-style questions.

STYLE REQUIREMENTS (based on analysis of real Tunisian jurist writing)

1. CITATION FORMAT — Use authentic Tunisian legal citation:
   - Codified law: "article X du [Code abbreviation]" (e.g., "article 2 du COC", 
     "article 96 du CSC")
   - Ranges: "articles X et suivants" or "articles X à Y"
   - Combined: "articles X et Y du [Code]"
   - End each substantive answer with a source line: "Sources: art. X, Y du [Code]"
   - Arabic citation: "الفصل X من [اسم المجلة]" (e.g., "الفصل 2 من مجلة الالتزامات 
     والعقود")

2. ANSWER STRUCTURE (principe + exception + finalité):
   For substantive questions, structure answers in three movements:
   (a) State the principle with citation
   (b) Acknowledge significant exceptions, nuances, or related provisions
   (c) Briefly explain the purpose/rationale of the rule
   Use legal connectors: "En vertu de", "En outre", "Par ailleurs", "Toutefois", 
   "Ainsi", "En effet" / "بمقتضى", "علاوة على ذلك", "غير أن", "وبالتالي"

3. ANSWER LENGTH:
   - Simple factual questions: 50–100 words
   - Substantive questions (principle + exception): 150–250 words
   - Refusal/clarification: 40–80 words

4. DISCLAIMERS — Include in consumer-safe form on substantive answers:
   - French: "Ceci ne constitue pas un avis juridique. Pour votre situation 
     spécifique, consultez un avocat."
   - Arabic: "هذا لا يشكل استشارة قانونية. لوضعك الخاص، يُرجى استشارة محامٍ."

5. GROUNDING — Answers must be STRICTLY grounded in the provided article text.
   - Do NOT invent article numbers
   - Do NOT cite doctrine or jurisprudence that isn't in the retrieved text
   - If uncertain about any article number or content, add a "confidence_flag" 
     field to that example noting the uncertainty

6. REFUSAL BEHAVIOR — For refusal examples:
   "Les documents fournis ne contiennent pas d'informations suffisantes pour 
   répondre à cette question. Je vous recommande de consulter un avocat spécialisé 
   ou [relevant authority]."
   Arabic equivalent in natural phrasing.
